📖 Reading

One story, many questions

🎬 The secret of good interviews

Interviewers love stories. A story is proof. Prepare a few strong stories about your work, and you can use each one to answer many different questions.
proof = something that shows a thing is true · prepare = get ready before
My story: The morning the office printer broke, one hour before a big client presentation, I found a way to finish on time.

Which of these questions could this story answer? There are 3 in total.

🧠 What is the interviewer thinking?

a. Are this person’s salary hopes realistic?
b. How does this person react to feedback?
c. What is this person’s attitude to the old job and boss?
d. Did this person research us? Do they really care?
e. Who is the real person outside of work?
f. What do other people think of this person’s work?
criticism = saying what is wrong with your work · current = now · realistic = possible, not a dream · attitude = the way you think and feel about something

🎤 Speaking

Read the interviewer’s mind

The question is: “Why are you leaving your current job?” First, decide if each answer is good. Then click the button to read the interviewer’s thoughts.

🧑‍💼 Candidate: My boss was terrible and the pay was awful.
If this person speaks like that about the old boss, what will they say about us? Hmm…
🧑‍💼 Candidate: I learned a lot in my last job, and now I’m ready for more responsibility.
Positive, honest and ambitious. I like this answer.
🧑‍💼 Candidate: I don’t know. I just wanted a change.
No plan, no reason. Is this person serious about this job?
ambitious = wants to succeed and grow · responsibility = a duty or a job that you are in charge of
Now give your own answer. You may not say anything negative about your old job.
